---
title: Privacy model
description: Room text is double-encrypted. A live session does not show your IP.
---

Get NowHere is built to keep a room private. There is no chat server, no
phone number, and no open websocket of your conversation on the public
internet. Words are sealed on your device, then sealed again on the live
path. Outsiders do not get the room handed to them.

## Who can read the room

The person you invited. That is the point.

A third party does not. Not a chain explorer without the view keys. Not an
ISP. Not a DHT node. Not a “chat host,” because there isn’t one.

Someone who stole your wallet or already owns the phone can read what you
can read. That is a stolen identity, not a network design.

## Live session and IP

You add people the way you would in real life: you meet, you exchange
QR codes, then you open a room. Those two people already decided to talk.

A live session does **not** show your IP. Either counterpart could, with
dedicated software, extract the other person’s IP. They already chose to
talk to each other.

That is not a hole. Room text is still double-encrypted. It does not ride
`ws://`. It does not sit in plaintext on a server.

If you are not on a live session, relationship signaling and fallback
relay ride Conceal smart messages (ChaCha12). Outsiders without the view
keys still do not read the body, and that path does not hand your IP to
the other person at all.

## Two paths, same rule for the words

| Path | What it is for | What outsiders get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live peer-to-peer | Fast room when both sides can punch | Nothing. They would have to steal the room ID *and* the room topic just to land on ciphertext. Good luck. And then only the IP could be extracted. |
| Conceal (invites and fallback) | Meet, invite, and keep talking when the punch is down | Nothing — unless they stole the keys. |

Both paths exist so privacy and delivery can both be true. The live path
is sealed with **ChaCha20-Poly1305** on top of the transport seal. The
chain path uses Conceal message encryption. Neither is a public inbox.
